Abstract

Surgeon-centric operating room (OR) monitoring aims to reconstruct and track the motions and interactions of surgical teams, which can actively support downstream tasks including identity recognition, action interpretation and phase reasoning. However, existing approaches heavily rely on the appearance patterns encoded from general visual feature extractors, which could be challenged by OR environments where sterile clothing reduces appearance differences and frequent interaction raises occlusions.

Therefore, we present Geometry-guided Surgeon Reconstruction and Tracking (GeoSuRT), a new framework that leverages articulated skeletal topologies to instruct surgeon state reasoning. Its Skeleton Graph Lifting Network (SkeGLNet) learns multi-scale joint graph features to quantify person-specific visual relations, then selectively fuses fine-grained visual and geometry representations to regress human parameters. Its Geometry-Assisted Tracking (GeoTrack) constructs geometry-based spatiotemporal memory, which is integrated with local continuity analysis to constitute a unified online identity association mechanism. These contributions provide biological structural evidences to GeoSuRT when visual clues are absent or ambiguous, and make it more suitable for complicated OR environments.

Comprehensive evaluations are conducted on three datasets including laboratory, synthetic and public clinical scenes. Compared with the state-of-the-art methods, SkeGLNet reduces mesh vertex errors by 10.7%, 7.5% and 26.8%, respectively, with only 52.5% of network parameters. On long term tracking, GeoTrack improves composite tracking accuracy HOTA by 8.01% and reduces identity switches IDSW by 76.9%. These results demonstrate that GeoTrack is capable of supporting downstream surgeon monitoring tasks and facilitating intelligent OR systems of the next generation.

Monocular 3D reconstruction

SkeGLNet: Surgeon Mesh Recovery

SkeGLNet encodes multi-scale skeletal graphs and uses joint geometry to refine person-specific visual features. Cascaded cross-attention fuses geometric and visual representations to regress MHR pose, shape, and 3D position for surgeon mesh recovery.

Online multi-surgeon tracking

GeoTrack: Geometry-Assisted Tracking

GeoTrack combines 3D spatial continuity with causal pose memory to reconnect surgeon identities after occlusion. Geometry-based retrieval and joint reassignment maintain online identity associations without an additional appearance-based re-identification encoder.
